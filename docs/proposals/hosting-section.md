## 1. Goal

The operator wants "what runs on my machines and how it is reached" in one place. Today these parts are in three places:

- The containers of one machine and the choice Catalogue are in the tab Containers of the device page (tasks H3, H9 and H38 of the plan).
- The routes, with their names and certificates, are a section of Settings, in the group Hub (task H21).
- An application of the catalogue has no page of its own, because the hub keeps no record of the applications yet (task H6).

This proposal puts these parts in one section of the main navigation, named Hosting. Hosting is in the manner of Coolify and Arcane. Anima does not host the real Arcane program: the ruling of 2026-11-22 withdraws R-245 and R-247. The one-click catalogue (R-150, R-299) is not changed.

## 2. What Coolify and Arcane show

The operator's standing rule is that "emulate X" means copy the real screens of X first. These facts are read from their current documents and source, on 2026-11-22.

Three points follow from the two products:

- A domain belongs to the application that it serves (Coolify).
- The applications are shown by machine: Coolify groups them under its servers, and Arcane lists all agents together with a filter. The operator chose the groups of Coolify (R-427).
- The catalogue is a gallery with search and categories (both).

## 3. The requirements

- **HSE-001. A section Hosting in the main navigation.** The bar has a destination Hosting, with the icon of a box, directly below Code and above Agent usage (R-427, OD-HSE1). Where Code is not shown (a browser has no Code), Hosting is directly below Devices. Its count is the number of applications and routes that need the operator. The name is Hosting (R-428). Hosting has a list of sections on the left, as Settings has (R-426, R-428), in this order: Applications, with the Catalogue as a page below it, Models, Storage, Routes and Machines. A section has its own address (`/hosting`, `/hosting/catalogue`, `/hosting/models`, `/hosting/storage`, `/hosting/routes`, `/hosting/machines`), so that a link and the back button open the same section.
- **HSE-002. The section Applications.** The applications and the containers are rows of one list (R-426). An application of the catalogue and a plain container are rows of the same list. An application of several containers is one row that opens to its containers. A container that Anima did not install is marked Not managed and is read only (stage 1). The screen of stage 1 is this list, and stage 2 adds rows and actions to it; there is no second screen. The rows are in one group for each machine of the hub, as Coolify shows its servers (R-427, OD-HSE3). The page shows all machines together; the choice Machine narrows it to one. The header row of a group has the name of the machine, its state and the number of its rows. A machine with nothing installed is one short row with its name, "Nothing installed" and the link Install from the catalogue; it is not a full empty section. The columns of a row are the application or the container (its name, what it is and the number of its containers), the state, Reached at (HSE-011) and Updates. A field finds an application or a container by name across all machines; a group with no match is hidden while the field has text. A choice shows All, Needs you or Not managed. A thing that runs on a cluster is in the group of the cluster (HOST-052 of the plan; [clusters.md](clusters.md)). A row that needs the operator (stopped, not all running, a route that is lost or that waits) has the wash of its status, as in Routes. The button "Install from the catalogue" opens the Catalogue.
- **HSE-003. The page of one application.** The header has the name, the state with the machine, and the buttons Restart, Stop and Update when an update exists. The tabs follow the order of the settings of a resource in Coolify, with only what Anima has: General, Domains, Environment, Storage, Logs, Containers, Remove. Containers lists the containers of the project, with the rows of the tab Containers of the device page. Remove is last, as the Danger Zone of Coolify.
- **HSE-004. The Catalogue.** A page below Applications in the list of sections. The entries of the catalogue (task H36) as a list with categories on the left and a search field, in rows as the Library of the models (HSE-022). Version 5 drew a gallery of cards in the manner of the Templates of Arcane. A card has the name, one line about the entry, its category, and whether it is installed and on which machine, or "Not tested on a machine". Details opens the page that task H38 has. Install is shown, and it is off with its reason until task H6, as the hub refuses it today (H38).
- **HSE-005. The section Routes.** The section Routes of Settings moves to this section with no change to its frames (routes mockup, frames 1 to 4), its filters, Check all and Add a route. The target column names the application and is a link to its page (HSE-003). A route to a redirect has no application and stays as it is.
- **HSE-006. Certificates.** A certificate stays in the row and on the page of its route. Hosting has no tab of certificates. The hub is the one proxy, as the server proxy is in Coolify, and a certificate exists only for a public route. A certificate that ends soon puts its route in Needs you (CHK-007).
- **HSE-007. Logs.** The logs of an application are the tab Logs of its page (HSE-003), as in Coolify. Hosting has no tab of logs. The audit entries of the hub stay in the logs panel and the page Logs.
- **HSE-008. The tab Containers of the device page.** There is no second list of containers (R-426). The tab shows the list of HSE-002 narrowed to that machine, with a link to Hosting. The volumes are in the section Storage (HSE-015); the images and the networks stay in the tab. It loses the choice Catalogue, which moves to HSE-004.
- **HSE-009. Before the record of the applications.** Until the hub keeps the record of the applications of a machine (task H6), the section Applications shows the projects of Compose that the hub reads from each machine (task H8), with no Reached at column. It says why in one line. Catalogue and Routes are as they are built today.
- **HSE-010. Routes leaves Settings.** The group Hub of Settings no longer has a section Routes (R-427, OD-HSE2). The jump field of Settings still finds "Routes" and opens the section Routes of Hosting. The old address `/settings#routes` opens the same tab.
- **HSE-011. A route beside its application.**
  - In the list of applications, the column Reached at shows each route of the application with the dot of its state. A name is a link to the page of the route.
  - An application with no route says "Only on the machine" and has the link Add a route.
  - On the page of the application, the tab Domains lists its routes with the port, the state of the proof and the certificate, and what to do. Its button "Add a route to <name>" opens Add a route with the application already chosen. This is Frame 6 of the routes mockup.
- **HSE-013. The section Models.** The models that run on the machines (the model service of [compute-plan.md](../plans/compute-plan.md)), grouped by machine as HSE-002. That plan owns what a row of a model holds and what it does; this section is only its place in Hosting.
- **HSE-014. Where a thing runs.** A row names the place where it runs, not a device identifier: a machine today, a cluster later (R-426). HOST-052 of [hosted-applications-plan.md](../plans/hosted-applications-plan.md) owns this notion; the screen shows the name of the place.
- **HSE-015. The section Storage.** The volumes of each place, grouped as HSE-002: the name, the application or the container that uses it, the size, and the place. A volume that no row uses is marked. The model files are the model service's (HSE-013) and are not listed here. Stage 1 reads; stage 2 adds the actions of a volume (remove, copy to a different machine) through the work route.
- **HSE-016. The section Machines.** The places where things can run: each machine that can host (its engine, its room in processor, memory and disk, whether it gave its consent to host a public route, OD-HOST-43) and each cluster with its members ([clusters.md](clusters.md)). A machine can be in several clusters, and its row shows each of them (R-423, CLU-015). Devices stays the page of a device for access and checks; a machine row here links to it. The owner of the machine side (what a machine reports, how it joins) is the OS stream ([spare-computer-os.md](spare-computer-os.md)).
- **HSE-017. The load and the room of each machine.** The section Machines (HSE-016) shows, for each machine, its state, processor, memory and disk, and VRAM when it has a GPU, each as a meter with the percentage, from the metrics of the device that the Devices list shows. A row also gives the number of its applications, models and volumes, and the time of the last read. The meters and their colour levels are those of the Devices list, in each section of Hosting. The free room for a new thing stays with the OS stream (HSE-016). SV1, R-NEW-serverosall.
- **HSE-018. The Hosting page of one machine.** The name of a machine in Hosting opens its page at `/hosting/machines/<machine>`. The header has the name, the state and a link to the device page. Tiles give the processor, memory, disk and VRAM now, with a line of the last hour and its peak. Below them are the applications, models, volumes and routes of that machine, in the rows of their sections. The actions are Install here (the picker of HSE-022 with this machine chosen) and Pull a model here (the dialog of Frame 8). An action that is off says why. SV2, R-NEW-serverosall.
- **HSE-019. The overview of Hosting.** The top of Hosting has four numbers: machines up, applications running, routes proven, and models ready. The word under a number changes colour only when something needs the operator. A number opens its section with Needs you chosen. Below the numbers, each thing that needs the operator has one line with one action. With nothing that needs the operator, the list is one line that says so. SV4, R-NEW-serverosall.
- **HSE-020. Logos.** A catalogue entry and a row of an application have the brand mark of the program, from Simple Icons (CC0), matched on the entry or on the name of the image, in the accent colour, as the Library of the models draws a publisher. With no mark, the row has the first letter. No mark is taken from a product that the licence of Simple Icons does not cover. SV6, R-NEW-serverosall.
- **HSE-021. One toolbar.** Each list of Hosting (Applications, Models, Storage, Routes, Machines) has one toolbar in one order: pills with counts, a search field that the key `/` opens, Machine, Sort (name, processor, memory, where the list has them), then the main action. A sort stays as it is when the list reads again. The list shows the time of its last read beside Refresh. A list reads again by itself while a thing in it changes, and stops while the window is hidden. SV7, R-NEW-serverosall.
- **HSE-022. The Catalogue as a list and a picker.** The Catalogue (HSE-004) is a list of rows: the logo (HSE-020), the name, one line, the category, and the machines where it is installed. The same list opens as a picker from Install here on a machine (HSE-018), with that machine chosen; the arrow keys move in it and Enter opens an entry. Install stays off with its reason until task H6. SV9, R-NEW-serverosall.
- **HSE-023. The fit of a catalogue entry on each machine.** The Catalogue (HSE-022) gives, for each machine, whether an entry can run there and the reason in words: no container engine, a port that the entry needs is in use, not enough memory or disk, or a different processor type. The words are in the manner of the fit of a model in the Library. Install chooses the machine with the best fit; the operator can change the machine. SV3, R-NEW-serverosall.
- **HSE-024. The use of each application.** Applications (HSE-002) shows the processor and memory of each application, as the sum of its containers, and of each container, its uptime with the number of restarts, and its published ports. A row of an application of several containers adds up its rows. The meters are those of HSE-017. SV5, R-NEW-serverosall.
- **HSE-025. An install that runs is a row.** An install from the Catalogue is a row at the top of its machine group (HSE-002) until the machine reports the application: Waiting, Pulling, Starting, or Failed with its reason and its log. It has the form of a pull of a model (HSE-013). SV8, R-NEW-serverosall.
- **HSE-026. A removal says what it loses.** Remove of an application (HSE-003), and of a volume that holds data (HSE-015), asks the operator to type the name. The text says what goes and what stays, names the volumes that go, and differs for an application that Anima did not install. SV10, R-NEW-serverosall.
- **HSE-012. The phone layout.** Hosting is a destination of the phone layout in the place that the owner of the phone navigation chooses (mobile plan). On a phone, the list of sections is the first screen of Hosting, as the list of Settings is, and the list of applications shows the name and the state under the header of its machine, with the routes below the name.

## 4. Where Routes goes

Routes becomes the last section of Hosting (HSE-005). It leaves Settings (HSE-010) because one fact has one place (PLN-016), and because the operator asked for it in Hosting. Settings keeps a way to find it through the jump field, not a second copy.

A route shows beside the application that it serves in two places: the column Reached at of the list (Frame 1) and the tab Domains of the application (Frame 2). The full page of a route, with its DNS records and its checks, stays in Routes, so that it has one owner.
